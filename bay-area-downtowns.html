On sourcing and confidence. Coordinates are main-street or civic-core reference points for trip planning, not district boundaries — for neighborhood entries they point at the commercial strip rather than the neighborhood centroid. Founding histories are compiled from general historical knowledge; a handful of the more unusual claims were checked against public sources (Menlo Park's 1867 depot, the Hercules powder works, Niles' Essanay film era, and Santa Clara's 1964 downtown demolition). Treat exact years as approximate, and "Origin story" as the dominant driver among what were usually several overlapping causes.

Timing notes are starting points, not gospel. They reflect street orientation, known markets and events, and the character of each place — verify current market days and theater marquee hours before making a trip. A place's origin and its present-day verdict are separate questions: Hercules was a genuine company town but reads as "Invented" today because almost nothing of that history survives above ground.
